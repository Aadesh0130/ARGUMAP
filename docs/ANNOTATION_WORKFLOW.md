## 1. End-to-End Annotation Workflow

The transformation of raw spoken discourse transcripts into gold-standard argumentative graphs follows a rigorous 9-stage sequence:

```
[1. Source Debate Transcript] (e.g., QT30, US2016, or academic debate)
              │
              ▼
[2. Speaker & Turn Segmentation] (Extract sequential turns, speaker metadata, side/role)
              │
              ▼
[3. Unit Proposition Segmentation] (Clause-level division of multi-sentence utterances)
              │
              ▼
[4. Component Annotation] (Independent labeling into 6 frozen component classes)
              │
              ▼
[5. Relation Annotation] (Independent link prediction across viable candidate pairs)
              │
              ▼
[6. Second Annotator Review] (Double-blind annotation of identical debate partitions)
              │
              ▼
[7. Agreement Measurement] (Compute Cohen's / Fleiss' Kappa: kappa_comp >= 0.70, kappa_rel >= 0.65)
              │
              ▼
[8. Adjudication Session] (Formal review of disagreements with documented tie-breakers)
              │
              ▼
[9. Final Gold Data Export] (Deterministic debate-level train/val/test serialization)
```

### Stage Details
1. **Source Debate Transcript:** Raw UTF-8 dialogue transcripts verified for clear speaker attribution and audio/text alignment.
2. **Speaker & Turn Segmentation:** Normalizes turn boundaries, speaker identifiers, roles (e.g. *Debater*, *Moderator*), and stances (*Affirmative*, *Negative*).
3. **Unit Proposition Segmentation:** Annotators isolate discrete argumentative propositions. Complex clauses connected by discourse connectives (*because*, *therefore*, *however*) are segmented into independent units with non-overlapping character offsets.
4. **Component Annotation:** Each unit is assigned exactly one label from the frozen 6-component taxonomy (`CLAIM`, `PREMISE`, `EVIDENCE`, `COUNTERARGUMENT`, `REBUTTAL`, `OTHER`) with a confidence rating ($0.0 \le c \le 1.0$).
5. **Relation Annotation:** Annotators examine ordered pairs $(u_{\text{source}}, u_{\text{target}})$ generated by the candidate window and assign directed relations (`SUPPORTS`, `ATTACKS`, `RESPONDS_TO`, `NO_RELATION`).
6. **Second Annotator Review:** In the pilot and benchmark batches, at least two annotators independently tag identical transcripts without cross-consultation.
7. **Agreement Measurement:** Compute multi-class Cohen's Kappa ($\kappa$) for pairs of annotators or Fleiss' Kappa ($\kappa$) for groups.
8. **Adjudication Session:** Disagreements are brought before an adjudication panel led by a principal computational linguist. Resolving choices are logged with formal rationale.
9. **Final Gold Data Export:** Validated with `validate_annotations.py` and exported to `data/annotations/gold/`.

## 2. Proposed Annotation Pilot

Before committing annotator resources to hundreds of debate hours, an **Annotation Pilot** will be conducted.

### 2.1 Pilot Setup
* **Volume:** 2 to 3 representative full debates (~150–250 argument propositions total).
* **Annotator Team:** Minimum 2 independent annotators plus 1 senior adjudicator.
* **Corpus Diversity:**
  * 1 two-party presidential/adversarial debate (e.g. from `US2016`).
  * 1 multi-party panel deliberation (e.g. from `QT30`).
  * 1 collegiate/academic debate.

### 2.2 Pilot Success Criteria & Gatekeeping
> [!IMPORTANT]
> The annotation pilot determines whether the current guidelines are sufficiently reliable for machine learning. Production dataset annotation will NOT begin until the pilot achieves:
> * Component label agreement: Fleiss' $\kappa \ge 0.70$.
> * Relation label agreement: Fleiss' $\kappa \ge 0.65$.

If agreement falls below these thresholds, a **Disagreement Analysis** will be performed, followed by guideline calibration, before repeating the pilot on 1 additional debate.

## 3. Handling Known Annotation Ambiguities

During dataset research, six recurring dialectical ambiguities were identified. The rules below represent the current working hypotheses of the project team, but they are explicitly designated as:

> **STATUS: PROVISIONAL**  
> *(To be validated, stress-tested, and refined during the Annotation Pilot. These rules must NOT be treated as unchangeable dogma until verified by pilot agreement data.)*

### 3.1 `CLAIM` vs. `PREMISE` (PROVISIONAL)
* **The Ambiguity:** In extended argumentation, an intermediate conclusion acts as both a claim (needing support) and a premise (supporting an overarching thesis).
* **Provisional Rule:**
  * If an utterance expresses the central contention of the speaker's speech or turn, tag as `CLAIM`.
  * If an utterance is subordinate and directly answers *"Why is the thesis true?"*, tag as `PREMISE`.
  * *Pilot Validation Test:* Annotators will log instances where statements function in chained syllogisms ($A \rightarrow B \rightarrow C$).

### 3.2 `PREMISE` vs. `EVIDENCE` (PROVISIONAL)
* **The Ambiguity:** Deductive reasoning vs. factual substantiation.
* **Provisional Rule:**
  * Tag as `PREMISE` if the proposition provides logical mechanisms, causal principles, or conceptual theories.
  * Tag as `EVIDENCE` only if the proposition cites verifiable external data, statistics, named studies, official reports, historical dates, or legal precedents.
  * Generalized anecdotal assertions (*"Everyone knows prices are up"*) remain `PREMISE`, not `EVIDENCE`.

### 3.3 `COUNTERARGUMENT` vs. `REBUTTAL` (PROVISIONAL)
* **The Ambiguity:** Both components oppose an opponent's case, but at different dialectical depths.
* **Provisional Rule:**
  * `COUNTERARGUMENT` is an **offensive objection** that initiates an attack against an established primary case (Depth 1 attack: Negative attacks Affirmative thesis).
  * `REBUTTAL` is a **defensive recovery or counter-attack** made in response to a counterargument (Depth 2 defense: Affirmative neutralizes Negative counterargument).
  * *Pilot Validation Test:* Track if annotators can reliably identify dialectical turn depth in fast-paced cross-examination.

### 3.4 `COUNTERARGUMENT` vs. `CLAIM` (PROVISIONAL)
* **The Ambiguity:** When the negative speaker opens their constructive case, does their main contention count as a constructive `CLAIM` or a `COUNTERARGUMENT`?
* **Provisional Rule:**
  * If the proposition introduces an independent affirmative thesis for the negative team (*"We should adopt a carbon tax instead"*), tag as `CLAIM`.
  * If the proposition exists solely to deny or expose flaws in the opponent's case (*"Their plan will bankrupt the treasury"*), tag as `COUNTERARGUMENT`.

### 3.5 `SUPPORTS` vs. `RESPONDS_TO` (PROVISIONAL)
* **The Ambiguity:** Explanatory or clarifying statements that provide context without formal deductive support.
* **Provisional Rule:**
  * Tag as `SUPPORTS` if $u_{\text{source}}$ directly increases the logical likelihood or evidential backing of $u_{\text{target}}$.
  * Tag as `RESPONDS_TO` if $u_{\text{source}}$ clarifies, rephrases, or contextualizes $u_{\text{target}}$ without adding evidential or deductive weight.

### 3.6 `ATTACKS` vs. `RESPONDS_TO` (PROVISIONAL)
* **The Ambiguity:** Polite or partial concessions, rhetorical questions, and nuanced reframing.
* **Provisional Rule:**
  * Tag as `ATTACKS` if $u_{\text{source}}$ seeks to falsify, contradict, or invalidate $u_{\text{target}}$.
  * Tag as `RESPONDS_TO` if $u_{\text{source}}$ addresses the opponent's point through partial concession (*"While that is true, we must consider..."*), asks a procedural question, or shifts the conversational frame without direct logical refutation.

## 4. Candidate-Pair Strategy & Distance Window ($k$)

### 4.1 Combinatorial Pair Explosion
In a debate with $N$ units, exhaustive pairwise evaluation requires $N(N - 1)$ pairs. For a 100-unit debate, this generates 9,900 pairs, over 95% of which have no relationship. 

### 4.2 Candidate Generation Strategy
Candidate pairs $(u_i, u_j)$ are restricted by:
1. **Directional Chronology:** An argument typically targets an earlier statement ($Turn(u_i) \ge Turn(u_j)$).
2. **Turn Distance Window ($k$):** Pairs are evaluated only if $|Turn(u_i) - Turn(u_j)| \le k$ turns.
3. **Unlinked Pairs Receive `NO_RELATION`:** Any candidate pair within window $k$ that lacks an active edge is assigned the label `NO_RELATION`.

### 4.3 Setting the Value of $k$
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **VALUE OF $k$: EMPIRICALLY CALIBRATED (PROVISIONAL $k = 3$)**  
> Following Phase 1 pilot empirical calibration across authentic debates (UK Hansard AI 2024, US 2016 Presidential, Kennedy-Nixon 1960), the 95th percentile of observed positive argument links is reached at **$k = 3$ turns** (capturing 97.06% of links with directional filtering).
>
> Full calibration statistics and workload simulations are documented in [docs/RELATION_WINDOW_CALIBRATION.md](file:///c:/Users/hii/Downloads/ARGUMAP/docs/RELATION_WINDOW_CALIBRATION.md).

**Provisional Protocol for Phase 2 Expanded Pilot:**
1. **Candidate Set Generation:** For each transcript, candidate pairs are deterministically filtered by:
   $$0 \le Turn(u_{\text{source}}) - Turn(u_{\text{target}}) \le 3 \quad \text{and} \quad u_{\text{source}} \neq u_{\text{target}}$$
2. **Directionality:** Intra-turn pairs ($Turn_s = Turn_t$) are evaluated bidirectionally (since empirical data shows 13.0% of intra-turn links are forward-directed premises). Cross-turn pairs ($Turn_s > Turn_t$) enforce the chronological arrow of speech.
3. **Explicit 4-Class Labeling:** Annotators must explicitly assign one of the 4 frozen labels (`SUPPORTS`, `ATTACKS`, `RESPONDS_TO`, `NO_RELATION`) for every candidate pair in the window. Pairs lacking an argument relationship are explicitly tagged as `NO_RELATION`.
4. **Out-of-Window Overrides:** Annotators may optionally flag an argumentative link targeting an earlier turn ($k > 3$) as a long-range override without evaluating the entire combinatorial space.
5. **No Premature Freezing:** Because the Phase 1 pilot is small (3 debates, 34 links), $k = 3$ remains strictly provisional until stress-tested across the expanded Phase 2 pilot corpus.
